Research

The research of our group, RoSI, spans three complementary layers of intelligence.

Core intelligence

  • Machine learning and AI for robotics Learning algorithms that let robots learn from data and improve adaptively.
  • Robotic perception and sensing Multimodal sensor data (cameras, LiDAR, IMU, GPS) for perceiving the environment, recognising objects, and building maps.
  • Advanced control systems Nonlinear, model predictive, adaptive, and learning-based control for stable and safe robot motion.
  • Planning and decision making Task, path, and trajectory planning, and autonomous decision making under system constraints.

Embodied intelligence

  • Locomotion and autonomous navigation Robust mobility for wheeled and legged robots in real-world terrain.
  • Manipulation and grasping Robotic arms and end-effectors, including soft grippers, from grasp planning to force control.

Social intelligence

  • Multi-robot systems and fleet management Coordination, task allocation, and communication for logistics, inspection, and service applications.
  • Human–robot interaction Safe and intuitive interfaces, collaboration modes, and supervision.

Projects

01

Sensor fusion · State estimation

Sensor fusion and localisation

Reliable localisation is a prerequisite for autonomous ground vehicles, and it has to work with sensors that are affordable. This work fuses wheel odometry, inertial measurements, consumer-grade GPS, and RTAB-Map visual mapping through extended Kalman filtering, and evaluates the result on personal mobility vehicles in outdoor environments.

Related work covers indoor localisation that combines an indoor positioning system with odometry, and a survey of sensor fusion for GPS-aided localisation. My current work extends this line towards learning-based sensor fusion for state estimation.

02

Autonomous navigation · Personal mobility

Autonomous personal mobility

This research investigates perception and control for personal mobility vehicles, including visual-feedback docking, wheelchair localisation, and real-time lane detection on BRIN’s SEATER prototype. SEATER (Single-passenger Electric Autonomous Transporter) is a compact differential-drive vehicle for first- and last-mile mobility, developed in indoor (I-SEATER) and outdoor (O-SEATER) variants.

The docking and path-tracking studies use nonlinear model predictive control, while the lane-detection work evaluates learning-based perception on an embedded computing platform.

Field robotics · Multimodal sensing

Inspection and exploration robots

With the Robotics and System Intelligence (RoSI) research group at BRIN, I work on mobile robots for inspection and exploration, including quadruped platforms and multimodal sensor payloads. This is ongoing work.

04

Rescue robotics · Model predictive control

Autonomous casualty extraction

ResQbot explores the use of mobile robots to approach and extract casualties. My work on hierarchical decomposed-objective model predictive control (HiDO-MPC) coordinates the motion objectives involved in autonomous casualty approach.

The research combines simulation and physical robot experiments to evaluate control accuracy and robustness. Related work on ResQbot 2.0 investigates a stretcher-style design with an inflatable neck-securing device, evaluated through simulation. A review of rescue robots for casualty extraction was published in 2025.

05

Robot perception · Sim-to-real learning

Learning to detect casualties

Ground-projected point clouds provide a representation for detecting people lying on the ground. This work uses synthetic training data and augmentation to transfer a learned detector to real sensor observations.

The Deep_GPPC repository accompanies the research on sim-to-real learning for casualty detection.
