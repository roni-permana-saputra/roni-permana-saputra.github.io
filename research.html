Research

The research of our group, RoSI, spans three complementary layers of intelligence.

Core intelligence

  • Machine learning and AI for robotics Learning algorithms that let robots learn from data and improve adaptively.
  • Robotic perception and sensing Multimodal sensor data (cameras, LiDAR, IMU, GPS) for perceiving the environment, recognising objects, and building maps.
  • Advanced control systems Nonlinear, model predictive, adaptive, and learning-based control for stable and safe robot motion.
  • Planning and decision making Task, path, and trajectory planning, and autonomous decision making under system constraints.

Embodied intelligence

  • Locomotion and autonomous navigation Robust mobility for wheeled and legged robots in real-world terrain.
  • Manipulation and grasping Robotic arms and end-effectors, including soft grippers, from grasp planning to force control.

Social intelligence

  • Multi-robot systems and fleet management Coordination, task allocation, and communication for logistics, inspection, and service applications.
  • Human–robot interaction Safe and intuitive interfaces, collaboration modes, and supervision.

Current research programmes

Autonomous navigation · Personal mobility

Autonomous personal mobility and mobility-on-demand

We develop SEATER (Single-passenger Electric Autonomous Transporter), a compact electric vehicle for first- and last-mile travel inside campuses, office complexes, and other managed areas, in indoor (I-SEATER) and outdoor (O-SEATER) variants. The aim is an autonomous mobility-on-demand service that is affordable enough to deploy in Indonesia.

The work covers the whole vehicle: platform design, docking and path tracking with nonlinear model predictive control, lane detection and lane keeping with learned perception, and a virtual-reality environment for driver testing. A new collaboration with Shanghai Jiao Tong University extends this towards semantic urban mapping and multimodal intelligent transport.

Funding BRIN (2023, 2024), with Universitas Diponegoro; BRIN – LPDP – MOST China (2026), with Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

Sensor fusion · State estimation

Sensor fusion and localisation

A ground vehicle has to know where it is, reliably, and with sensors that are affordable. We fuse wheel odometry, inertial measurements, consumer-grade GPS, and visual mapping through extended Kalman filtering, and evaluate the result on personal mobility vehicles and mobile robots, indoors and outdoors.

Results include fusion-based localisation for outdoor personal mobility vehicles, a multi-sensor localisation system for low-cost indoor navigation, fusion of an indoor positioning system with odometry, and a survey of GPS-aided sensor fusion. My current work extends this line towards learning-based sensor fusion for state estimation.

Service robots · Multi-robot systems

Autonomous delivery and logistics robots

DELL-E is our last-mile delivery robot for delivery-on-demand in managed areas, developed as a complete system: the robot, its autonomy software, and the connected services around it. The work has produced a registered patent and an industrial design for the robot, and a companion delivery application.

In parallel, we study mobile robots for flexible manufacturing, including Mecanum-wheeled platforms and safe navigation with model predictive control and control barrier functions, and are extending this to multi-robot systems for smart manufacturing.

Funding BRIN (2025), with Universitas Diponegoro; BRIN (2026).

Field robotics · Multimodal sensing

Inspection and exploration robots

QUINS is a quadruped inspection system for industrial sites and high-risk zones, combining a legged platform with IoT connectivity and a multimodal sensor payload. Alongside the platform, we work on the perception a robot needs to understand such environments: semantic mapping, hazard mapping, and object detection with 360-degree cameras and 3D LiDAR.

Funding BRIN (2026), with Pertamina, Universitas Gadjah Mada, and Universitas Airlangga.

Manipulation · Assistive robotics

Manipulation and assistive robots

On the manipulation side, we work on position, force, and admittance control of a six-axis arm, and on vision-guided grasping. On the assistive side, we develop on-device tactile intelligence for low-cost prosthetic hands using bio-based stretchable sensors, and contribute to a lower-limb exoskeleton for physiotherapy assistance.

Funding Toray Science and Technology Research Grant (2025); BRIN – LPDP (2024), with Telkom University Surabaya.

Earlier work

Rescue robotics · Model predictive control

Autonomous casualty extraction

My doctoral research at Imperial College London developed ResQbot, a mobile rescue robot that approaches and extracts casualties. Hierarchical decomposed-objective model predictive control (HiDO-MPC) coordinates the motion objectives of the approach, and ResQbot 2.0 adds a stretcher-style design with an inflatable neck-securing device. A review of rescue robots for casualty extraction followed in 2025.

Funding Indonesia Endowment Fund for Education (LPDP) doctoral scholarship.

Robot perception · Sim-to-real learning

Learning to detect casualties

Ground-projected point clouds provide a representation for detecting people lying on the ground. This work uses synthetic training data and augmentation to transfer a learned detector to real sensor observations.
