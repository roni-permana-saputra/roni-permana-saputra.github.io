Research

My research explores how mobile robots perceive their surroundings, plan their motion, and interact with the physical world.

RESCUE ROBOTICS · MODEL PREDICTIVE CONTROL

Autonomous casualty extraction

ResQbot explores the use of mobile robots to approach and extract casualties. My work on hierarchical decomposed-objective model predictive control (HiDO-MPC) coordinates the motion objectives involved in autonomous casualty approach.

The research combines simulation and physical robot experiments to evaluate control accuracy and robustness. Related work on ResQbot 2.0 investigates a stretcher-style design with an inflatable neck-securing device, evaluated through simulation.

ROBOT PERCEPTION · SIM-TO-REAL LEARNING

Learning to detect casualties

Ground-projected point clouds provide a representation for detecting people lying on the ground. This work uses synthetic training data and augmentation to transfer a learned detector to real sensor observations.

The Deep_GPPC repository accompanies the research on sim-to-real learning for casualty detection.

AUTONOMOUS NAVIGATION · PERSONAL MOBILITY

Autonomous personal mobility

This research investigates perception and control for personal mobility vehicles, including visual-feedback docking, wheelchair localisation, and real-time lane detection on BRIN’s SEATER prototype.

The docking study uses nonlinear model predictive control, while the lane-detection work evaluates learning-based perception on an embedded computing platform.
